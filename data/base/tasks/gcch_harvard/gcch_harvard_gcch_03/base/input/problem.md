Thales Should NOT merge with Rheinmetall 
Why should Thales NOT merge with Rheinmetall ?
A leading European defense manufacturer specializing in armoured vehicles, 
munitions, and advanced land defense systems.
▪ Financial Disadvantage: A merger between Thales and Rheinmetall does 
not create value for Thales shareholders, because their ownership stake in 
the combined company makes it -€18.8bn less worth than Thales on a 
standalone basis (€85.6bn). 
▪ Strategic Dilution: As leader in "heavy metal“ (tanks/munitions). Merging 
would dilute Thales’ identity as a high-margin AI, cyber, & space specialist, 
which shifts the portfolio from 20% CAGR tech-segments back toward 
cyclical, low-margin industry.
▪ Political Deadlock: Dual 'National Asset' status in FR and DE makes a full 
merger politically unnavigable - government vetoes, regulatory delays and 
cultural misalignment are structural and not solvable. A disciplined JV 
captures the upside without the sovereign risk.

Why should Thales ACQUIRE Tekever ?
A fast-growing aerospace and defense technology company focused on 
autonomous UAV’s and real-time intelligence solutions.
▪ Market Dominance: Leader in European maritime UAS with a €30m EMSA 
contract and a central role in the €456m “Project OVERMATCH” , which 
anchors its position in the high-growth shipborne and maritime ISR-as-a-
Service market.
▪ Combat-Proven AI: 10,000+ flight hours in Ukraine providing "data-gold" 
for Thales’ cortAIx AI accelerator, which bypasses 5 years of R&D by 
acquiring battle-hardened autonomous target recognition (ATR) 
algorithms.
▪ Sovereignty Play: As a NATO Innovation Fund (NIF) backed unicorn, 
Tekever provides a protected IP moat against US-based disrupters, 
positioning Thales as the preeminent sovereign EU tech prime.
Recommendation 2 - Acquire Tekever at €1.2B to secure autonomous drone sovereignty & cortAlx AI data advantage. Deepen 
Rheinmetall JV (MGCS) for land-sector volume without structural merger risk. Together, these position Thales asEurope's sovereign 
defence-tech prime by 2030.
Roadmap to 2030: Two moves define Thales' future - a Tekever acquisition for AI and drone sovereignty and a deepened Rheinmetall 
JV for land-sector scale. Together, they accelerate Thales transition into the Sovereign EU Defence Tech Champion.

CONCLUSION
Thales should NOT merge with Rheinmetall
Thales gains more advantages with a JV with Rheinmetall rather than a 
full merger.
Financial: A Thales–Rheinmetall merger is not value-accretive for 
Thales’ shareholders because the intrinsic value of their pro-forma 
equity stake in the combined company would be below Thales’s 
standalone equity value by €18.8bn.
Strategic/Political: Merger destroys Thales’s identity as a technology 
leader. Government vetoes and long integration timelines drains focus 
from AI/cyber/space segments growing at 20% CAGR where Thales 
holds a clear competitive advantage.
Political deadlock: Germany views Rheinmetall as 
strategic asset for National security, while France 
treats Thales likewise. National sovereignty concerns 
make approval unlikely. 
Localization: Tied to their countries, heavy-industry 
plants and high-reliability electronics sites are highly 
specialized, which limits consolidation due to 
security-of-supply and offset obligations.
Opportunity costs: Capital lock-up and post merger 
integration focus (5-7 years) diverts focus from 
high-growth domains like AI software, autonomous 
segments etc. (greater than 20% CAGR)
Post merger integration: Strong cultural 
misalignment (state-influenced systems culture vs. 
agile, platform-centric model) and heavy regulatory 
scrutiny and mitigation agreements distract 
management amid booming standalone order books.
Thales has never executed a transaction above €5bn, making Rheinmetall, valued at over 15× that size which 
is far beyond its historical M&A scale.
