Fractional Follies Fall 2020 ARML Power Contest In this exploration, a program will be a list of positive fractions in lowest terms. A program can be applied to a starting number, which is a positive integer, resulting in another positive integer P (n). To apply program P = ( f1, f2, . . . , fk) to the integer n, determine the ﬁrst fraction in the list so that fin is an integer, and that integer is the result of the application. If none of the fractional multiplications produces an integer, then the result of the application is just the original number n. For example, let P = (2 5 , 15 7 , 3 4 ) . The result of applying P to 14 is 30, because 2 5· 14 is not an integer, but 15 7 · 14 = 30 is an integer. The result of applying P to 9 is 9, because none of the fraction in P yields an integer on multiplication by 9.
