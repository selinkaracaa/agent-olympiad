Zigzags and Triangles Fall 2021 ARML Power Contest The Background There are a number of ways in which triangular arrays of numbers lead to interesting pat- terns. Successful mathletes are certainly aware of Pascal’s triangle (also known as Tartaglia’s triangle, Yanghui’s triangle, and several others who studied it long before Pascal!). This con- test examines some other ways to combine numbers in a triangular fashion. Consider a sequence of real numbers{an}, which may be ﬁnite or inﬁnite. Its correspond- ing triangular array is created from the sequence as follows: Step 1: Place the sequence along the left-hand edge of a triangular array. Step 2: To compute any other entry in the array add the entry to its left and to its above-left. Step 3: Complete as many rows of the array as desired. The array will start as follows: a0 ↘ a1 −→ a0 +a1 ↘ ↘ a2 −→ a1 +a2 −→ a0 + 2a1 +a2 For a concrete example, for the sequence a0 = 0,a 1 = 1,a 2 = 2,... , the corresponding triangular array’s ﬁrst few rows will be 0 1 1 2 3 4 3 5 8 12 4 7 12 20 32 5 9 16 28 48 80 The entries in such a triangular array are numberedTn,k wheren is the row number, starting with zero, and k is the position from the left, also starting at zero. A diﬀerent way of using a triangular array to transform a sequence leads to some fasci- nating results. Instead of placing the sequence {an} on the left-hand edge of the triangular array, alternately place terms on the left- and right-hand edges. In rows where an has been placed on the left-hand edge, the entries to its right are computed in the above manner, adding the entry to the left and to the above left. In rows where an has been placed on the right, the process is backward, adding the entries to the right and above right. This triangular array is called the boustrophedon array and the resulting transformed sequence is called the boustrophedon transform of the original sequence. The word “boustrophedon” comes from the Greek roots bous, meaning “ox,” and strophos, meaning “turning,” and refers to the zigzag path that an ox takes while walking back and forth, plowing a ﬁeld. A permutation is called an up-down permutation if the numbers alternately increase and decrease, with the ﬁrst step being increasing. A down-up permutation has the numbers alternately increasing and decreasing with the ﬁrst step being decreasing. So ⟨⟨2, 4, 1, 3⟩⟩ is an up-down permutation while its reverse, ⟨⟨3, 1, 4, 2⟩⟩ is a down-up permutation. An alternating permutation is one that is either up-down or down-up. 2

Alternating permutations were ﬁrst studied by D´ esir´ e Andr´ e around 1880. This contest names the sequence{An} in his honor. The problem of determining the number of alternating permutations (which is, of course, 2 An by the results of these problems) is called Andr´ e’s problem. The numbers An are called Euler numbers, zigzag numbers, or up/down numbers. Be careful, because there are other sequences of numbers also called Euler numbers. The even- indexedA’s are also known as secant numbers, while the odd-indexed A’s are called tangent numbers. This is because they are related to the Maclaurin series for the secant and tangent functions. (Maclaurin series are studied in calculus.) These numbers are related to several other kinds of important numbers, such as Bernoulli and Stirling numbers. A neat result about these numbers that can be proven from their relationship to the secant and tangent functions is that when n is large, the ratio An/An+1 is very close to nπ/2—note that π/2 is the location of an asymptote of the secant and tangent functions! There are formulas for computing the terms of the boustrophedon transform or the inverse boustrophedon transform of a sequence directly in terms of the sequence, the up/down numbers, and the binomial coeﬃcients. More detailed information about these connections can easily be found by searching the web. 5
