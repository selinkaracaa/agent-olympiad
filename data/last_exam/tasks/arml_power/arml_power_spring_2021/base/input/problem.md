# Power Round — Spring 2021

Bipartitions and Beatty Sequences Spring 2021 ARML Power Contest The Background A partition of a set is simply a way of splitting the set into nonempty subsets, calledparts, that don’t overlap and that ﬁll up the whole set. For example, a partition of {1, 2, 3, 4, 5} could be{1, 3},{2, 5},{4}. A bipartition is speciﬁcally a partition into two parts. Given a subsetA of a setU, the setB of all elements ofU not belonging toA is called the complement ofA, denoted A′. So a subset A of U together with its complement A′ form a bipartition of U, provided neither A nor its complement A′ is empty. This contest will investigate some bipartitions of the positive integers. This contest frequently uses the ﬂoor and ceiling functions,⌊x⌋ and⌈x⌉ respectively. The ﬂoor function gives the greatest integer less than or equal to x while the ceiling gives the least integer greater than or equal to x. The following facts about the ﬂoor function may be used without further proof: • For any real number x,⌊x⌋≤ x< ⌊x⌋ + 1. • For any real number x and any integer n,⌊x +n⌋ =⌊x⌋ +n. The consequence of problem 3 is that X consists of a union of arithmetic sequences whose common diﬀerences are each equal to p, while the complementary set of all positive integers not in X is likewise a union of arithmetic sequences with common diﬀerence p. So the bipartitions of the positive integers created by looking at the whole part of multiples of a rational number are very predictable and boring. Not so for irrational numbers! For an irrational number x, the sequence{⌊x⌋,⌊2x⌋,⌊3x⌋,... } ={⌊kx⌋}∞ k=1 is called the Beatty sequence generated by x. 5. [2 pts.] It appears as if the sequence for φ2 ﬁlls in the gaps of the sequence for φ and vice versa (hint: if this isn’t the case for your answers to the previous two problems, go back and check your arithmetic!). Could it be that these two Beatty sequences are actually the complementary sets of a bipartition? First, note that φ2 = φ + 1, so φ is the positive solution to the polynomial equation x2−x− 1 = 0. 7.
